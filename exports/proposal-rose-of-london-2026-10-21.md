## Where you are now

**1. You are paying for production, not for growth**

Your producer edits the episodes and cuts reels, and the work gets done. But by your own estimate it costs about £2,000 for six episodes, he is busy, and you would like more from the process: ideas, reels and a second pair of eyes on what to make next. You are funding this from your own savings, with a mortgage alongside it, and you were honest about where it stands: "we're a very long way from it washing its face." Illustrative arithmetic, using only your figures: at roughly £2,000 per six episodes, that is about £333 an episode. Your tours start at £250, so every six episodes needs around eight bookings just to cover the edit. Right now nothing tells you whether a single booking came from the podcast. As you put it, "it needs to have a payoff."

**2. The podcast does not lead anywhere**

You record about two episodes a month, each 55 to 77 minutes, full of the knowledge that makes your tours worth booking. Then a listener finishes an episode and there is no clear next step to the tours or the website. The podcast and the business sit side by side instead of feeding each other. Every episode that goes out without a route to a booking is a chance to turn a listener into a £250 tour that simply passes by.

**3. YouTube is set up to be watched by people who already know you, not found by people who do not**

YouTube is a search engine, and it is now one of the main places AI search tools look for answers. People search it every day for history topics you could talk about in your sleep. But your thumbnails are the two of you, and episode topics are not yet chosen around what people are searching for. You said it yourself: "we're complete nobodies, and there's a lot of history podcasts out there." Without search led topics and thumbnails that earn a click, new listeners struggle to find you, however good the episode is.

**4. You are doing this on your own, without the numbers**

Mary is a trainee teacher with no spare time, so the thinking falls to you. Analytics are hard to get hold of, so you cannot see what is working. And you have no one to test ideas against: "it's an echo chamber." You posted your own reels and want to learn, but there is no one alongside you to show you how. The real risk is the one you named: "I don't want to keep using my savings if it's a lost cause." Without clear numbers, you cannot tell whether it is.

## What a good result looks like

You said you would like to grow it, and you are "very happy to pay if it's going to get me stuff in the long term." By the end of December, a good result is: six episodes that look and sound properly produced; episode topics chosen from what people actually search for; thumbnails people want to click; a steady flow of reels and Substack clips from every recording; your own login to the numbers; and a fortnightly conversation with someone who brings ideas. Then we sit down together, look at what the numbers say, and decide on the next step with clear eyes.

## What we will do

1. **Edit and produce six episodes, with ideas included** (problems 1 and 4). Full audio and video edit of each episode, five working days from receiving the files, one revision round, and every file yours to keep. I am a marketer as well as a podcast producer, so you get ideas with the editing, not just the editing.
2. **Turn each episode into a set of clips** (problems 2 and 3). From every episode: a 15 minute Substack cut and about four vertical reels with archive imagery and B-roll, so about 24 reels in all. Each one points back to the podcast and the tours, so the podcast becomes the single source of truth feeding Instagram, YouTube and your website.
3. **Make YouTube work as a search engine** (problem 3). I research the top 10 history topics people search for on YouTube now, and a fresh list to guide your next six episodes. Every episode gets a search led title, chapters and a new thumbnail designed to be clicked.
4. **Give you the numbers and a sounding board** (problem 4). You get your own analytics login, and we have a short ideas call every fortnight. I will show you what is working as we go, so your own reels get easier too.
5. **Optional add-on: Website and AI search boost** (problem 2). I make the changes on your WordPress site myself: page titles, descriptions, headings and the structure of your tour pages. I then write 10 well researched articles around what people search for when choosing a London tour, so your site is found when people compare London walking tour companies, and so AI search tools have something solid to cite.

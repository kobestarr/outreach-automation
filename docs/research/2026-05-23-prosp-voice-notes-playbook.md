# Prosp Voice Notes Playbook — UK Private Consultant Cardiologists

Research date: 2026-05-23
Sender: Kobi Omenaka (Kobestarr Digital)
Audience: UK private practice consultant cardiologists (Wave 1 / Wave 2 decision input)

---

## 1. How Prosp's voice clone actually works

Prosp's voice feature has **two distinct paths**, which the marketing copy conflates:

- **Pre-recorded broadcast voice note** — you record (or upload an MP3) once and the same audio is sent to every lead in the campaign. Only MP3 is supported. No personalisation.
- **AI Voice Cloner** (the "hyper-personalised" path, Tier 2+ on AppSumo) — you read an example script aloud for at least 30 seconds in the settings panel. Prosp captures your voice, then for each lead it takes a written script (with merge variables like first name, company, location) and generates that script in your cloned voice as the outbound audio. So it's **text → TTS-in-your-cloned-voice, per lead**, not a single re-used recording. Adjustable: stability, similarity, style, speaker boost.

Setup is "a couple of minutes" per Dave Swift's hands-on review, but he flagged the Chrome extension as flaky during testing and noted the cloned voice is **noticeably AI on close listen** — fine at conversational pace, weaker on consultant-grade audiences who listen attentively.

LinkedIn's hard ceiling: **60 seconds**, voice messages are **mobile-app only**, and **only to first-degree connections** (LinkedIn Help). That last constraint matters: a voice note cannot be the first touch — connection must land first.

## 2. High-converting voice-note format

Length consensus across Cleverly, Unkoa, Phantombuster, Pursue Networking (all 2025–26):

- LinkedIn allows 60s, **20–45s converts best**; Cleverly's tighter recommendation is **20–30s**.
- **First 5 seconds are decisive** (Cleverly) — name + a specific personal detail, not "Hi, I hope you're well."

Structure that recurs across sources:
1. **Personal hook** (specific detail from their profile, recent talk, hospital, publication) — 5s
2. **Reason for reaching out** tied to that hook — 10–15s
3. **Soft, low-friction ask** — "worth a 10-min chat?" / "want me to send the one-pager?" — 5s
4. **Always pair with a written follow-up text** that recaps and gives them a click path (Unkoa, Cleverly both insist on this).

Tonal markers Prosp's own tips article and Cleverly both stress: **smile while recording** (Prosp says "a smile comes through and is contagious"), conversational not scripted, mild imperfections welcome.

Never: lead with voice as the first touch; ramble to fill the time; pitch features in audio; use generic openers ("Hi, hope you're having a great week"); skip the text recap.

Reported numbers, with attribution:
- Prosp's own platform stat across 1,000+ campaigns: **47% reply rate on voice notes vs 8% on text** (Prosp help docs, Dave Swift). Treat as vendor-reported, not independently audited.
- Unkoa cites **+30–40% reply uplift** from a 30–45s voice note, attributed to Reply.io and Provoke Agency.
- Cleverly cites **up to +35%**.

The 47% figure is a marketing number from the vendor; the +30–40% practitioner figure is the safer planning anchor.

## 3. Where voice notes hit hardest in a sequence

Strong agreement across Unkoa, Cleverly, Zen-Mode, Prosp's default template:

- **Never first touch.** LinkedIn blocks it anyway (1st-degree only).
- **Best placement: step 2 or 3** — after connection accepted and one written DM has landed without a reply. The pattern break is the value, not the medium.
- Zen-Mode's specific cadence: voice at **Day 3** (first follow-up post-connect) and **Day 14** (soft close).
- **As a breakup message**, voice underperforms a short, warm written breakup according to Unkoa — the audio "ask" reads as desperate at the end of a cold sequence.

I did not find specific Daniel Disney / Justin Welsh / Lavender posts citing voice-note reply rates by sequence position — vendor blogs (Expandi, Cleverly, Cognism, Prosp) dominate the SERP. Treat the placement guidance as practitioner-blog consensus, not first-party data from named operators.

## 4. UK private consultant cardiologists — what actually lands

Direct medic-specific signal (Alpha Sophia):

- Physicians receive **~3x more inbox messages** than peers globally — assume saturated, default-to-delete.
- What kills outreach to them: length, generic subjects, feature-focused pitches, anything that smells of template.
- What earns reply: a specific reference to their work, publication or specialty; benefit framed against a practice-level problem; **educational content (a guideline summary, a third-party clinical data note)** rather than a pitch. "Become a resource, not a seller."
- Best send windows: **Tue/Thu, 10–11am or ~2pm**.

For a Kobestarr Digital website pitch to private consultant cardiologists specifically, the implications are:
- They are **not** the audience that finds a cloned-voice DM charming. Status-conscious, time-poor, sceptical of being "sold to" — an AI-flavoured voice is more downside than upside in Wave 1.
- The hook must be **clinic-specific**, not specialty-generic. "Saw your Harley St practice still lists 2023 fee schedule" beats "Saw you're a cardiologist."
- Soft CTA, no calendar link in the voice itself. Calendar link in the text recap.

## 5. Voice cloning ethics + disclosure

- **LinkedIn ToS**: the Professional Community Policies require disclosure for "synthetic or manipulated media that depicts a person saying something they did not say." A cloned-voice DM of *your own* voice saying words *you* authored is the awkward edge case — it depicts you saying things you didn't physically say. LinkedIn has not published explicit rules on cloned-voice DMs to date; the safest reading is that undisclosed cloned voice is at least policy-adjacent risk.
- **2025 regulatory direction**: AI-voice rules (esp. US robocall regime via FCC, and the May 2025 TAKE IT DOWN Act) are tightening fast. Mandatory disclosure within first 30s of AI-voice calls is now standard in the US robocall context. LinkedIn DMs aren't covered, but the direction of travel is one-way: toward disclosure.
- **Industry practice today**: Prosp's docs and Zen-Mode's playbook do **not** recommend disclosure. They lean on "modern voice cloning is good enough that recipients won't notice." That is exactly the posture regulators are moving against.
- **For a medical audience**: undisclosed AI-cloned voice in a DM to a consultant is a credibility own-goal if detected. The audience is precisely the demographic most likely to (a) listen carefully, (b) notice, (c) hold it against you. Kobestarr Digital's brand sits on craft and trust; even one consultant screenshotting "Kobi sent me an AI voice DM" to a peer group is more reputational cost than the campaign uplift is worth.

---

## Decision-relevant bottom line

**Do not use Prosp cloned-voice notes in Wave 1.** Two reasons stack:
1. LinkedIn restricts voice notes to first-degree connections — they cannot be the opener anyway, so Wave 1 (connection + first DM) doesn't have a slot for them.
2. The audience (UK consultant cardiologists) is the worst-case fit for AI-cloned audio: attentive listeners, status-sensitive, trust-driven, sceptical.

Voice notes are a **Wave 2 experiment** — after a connection is accepted and one written DM has gone unanswered, against a small (20–30 lead) test cell, using a **real recording** (not the clone), 25–35 seconds, smile-on, with a written recap and a hook referencing something clinic-specific. Hold the cloned-voice path back until there's evidence the audience responds to voice at all.
